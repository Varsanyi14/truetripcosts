# AUDIT: internal linking, Phase 1

**For:** MAIN + Gary, truetripcosts.com
**From:** OC
**Status:** audit and proposal. No links wired. One genuine 404 fixed, described in section 5.

Measured against the built site (`dist/`, 456 pages) rather than the source, so what is counted is what Google actually sees. Every figure below comes from parsing the shipped HTML with jsdom and classifying each anchor by the surface it sits on.

---

## 0. First, a correction to the working number

The brief says the site has about 199 internal links. The real figure is much larger, and the difference matters because it changes which problem we are solving.

| Measure | Count |
|---|---|
| Internal anchor instances across all 456 pages | 29,789 |
| Excluding nav and footer chrome | 12,130 |
| Unique page-to-page edges, excluding chrome | 9,248 |
| Of those, links that sit inside running prose | **1,468** |

So the site is not link-poor in volume. It is link-poor in the *one kind that carries weight*: 1,468 contextual edges against 9,248 total, and as section 2 shows, those 1,468 are distributed very unevenly. The 199 figure is probably a count of hand-authored `href`s in source, which undercounts because almost every link on this site is generated by a component from data.

**The real problem is not "too few links". It is that the structural layer is dense and points at the wrong things, while the contextual layer barely exists on the pages that need it.** That reframing changes the recommendation: the fix is mostly template-level and small-N, not a link-writing campaign.

Surface definitions used throughout, since the whole audit turns on them:

- **nav / footer** Site chrome. On every page. Near-zero ranking value.
- **card** A block link that is the whole clickable unit (a country door, a spoke card).
- **cta** A short link that is essentially the entire paragraph or table cell it sits in.
- **prose** A link inside a sentence with real text either side of it. This is the valuable one.

## 2. The high-value missing edges, ranked

Ranked by value per unit of work and per unit of dilution. Everything in tiers 1 and 2 is a template or data change affecting one file and yielding tens or hundreds of correct edges. **The tiers are a stopping guide: wire 1 and 2, then reassess before touching 3.** I have deliberately not proposed the 1,259 individual prose links the scan found, for the reason in section 2.4.

### Tier 1: one-line or one-component fixes with the largest effect

**1. Point the tipping hub at the tipping spokes.**
Source: `/tipping-compared`, via `src/components/TipChart.astro:80`.
Target: `/{country}/tipping` instead of `/{country}`.
Anchor: the country name, unchanged.
Why: 59 new edges onto pages with near-zero inbound, taken from the most over-linked page type on the site. A reader on a tipping comparison who clicks a country wants that country's tipping detail, not its money overview. Strictly more honest than the current target.

**2. Give every spoke an up-link to its topic hub.**
Source: `src/components/Spoke.astro`, in the existing sourcing or closing line where `/methodology` already sits.
Target: the spoke's topic hub, resolved from `topic`.
Anchor: the hub's own name, for example "how tipping works country by country" on a tipping spoke.
Why: 288 missing up-links across nine topics. The pets and rail spokes already do this, so there is a working precedent to copy rather than a new pattern to invent. This is the single biggest structural gap in the graph.
Caution: this must resolve from data and render plain text when no hub exists, or the four `vat-refund`, three `tourist-tax`, one `health`, one `entry-fees`, one `seasons` and one `all-inclusive` spokes will point at nothing. See section 4.

**3. Link the nine hotel-taxes spokes into the map, the hub, and the tourist-taxes hub.**
Source: the nine `/{country}/hotel-taxes-and-fees` spokes. Currently **none** of them links the map, the hub, the tourist-taxes hub, or the calculator.
Target: `/hotel-tax-map`, `/hotel-taxes-and-fees`, `/tourist-taxes`.
Anchor: "what other countries add on top of a room rate, mapped" for the map; the hub names for the hubs.
Why: the map's entire job is to pass authority to these spokes, and reciprocity is what makes a cluster read as a cluster rather than a hub-and-spoke funnel. This is the brief's freshest, most link-poor node and it is currently a dead end with one inbound link.

**4. Rescue the two orphan hubs.**
`/money-scams` and `/driving-and-car-rental` have zero non-chrome inbound links.
Fix via item 2 above (their 28 and 14 spokes gain up-links), plus one prose link each from the front door or a region hub.
Why: a hub reachable only from the nav cannot accumulate authority, and both have real spoke populations under them.

### Tier 2: small, high-value, needs a little editorial judgement

**5. Link the calculator from the spokes that discuss cost.**
Source: a targeted subset, not all 328. The tax, cash, and tipping spokes have the most natural claim.
Target: `/cost-comparison`.
Anchor: "what a day actually costs, compared".
Why: 0 of 328 spokes link the calculator today, and it holds 21 inbound edges total. This is the on-mission core the brief names, and it is nearly stranded. I would start with the 11 tax spokes and the 59 cash spokes and stop there to judge the effect.

**6. Front door to the calculator and the map.**
Source: `/` (91 outbound, none to either).
Target: `/cost-comparison`, `/hotel-tax-map`.
Anchor: the pages' own names.
Why: the front door is the strongest page on any site and it currently passes nothing to the two comparison tools.

**7. Region hubs down to a few spokes.**
Source: the six region hubs, which reach zero spokes.
Target: the two or three most relevant spokes per region, not all of them.
Anchor: the spoke's own subject.
Why: region hubs sit between the front door and the guides and pass nothing to the 328-page spoke layer. Keep this small; the guides already cover the countries.

**8. One cross-country spoke edge per topic, where it genuinely helps.**
Currently 1 such link exists site-wide. Example: a Schengen visa spoke referring to another Schengen country's, or a rail spoke referring to a neighbour's.
Why: this is real reader value, not chrome. Keep it to cases where the comparison is the point.

### Tier 3: the contextual prose layer, and why it is a trap

Scanning all 59 guides for prose that names a topic the country has a spoke for, with no prose link to it, returns **1,259 unlinked mentions**:

| Guides affected | Topic |
|---|---|
| 59 | cash or card |
| 47 | tipping |
| 36 | taxis and ride hailing |
| 34 | passports and visas |
| 12 | staying connected |
| 7 | hotel taxes and fees |
| 6 | money scams |
| 4 | driving and car rental |

Real examples, verbatim from the built pages:

- Vietnam guide: *"Tipping is not expected and there is no set percentage. Rounding up or leaving small notes..."* with no prose link to `/vietnam/tipping`. This is the brief's own hypothetical, and it is live.
- Japan guide: *"...an eSIM is usually cheaper and keeps you in control"* with no prose link to `/japan/staying-connected`.
- Thailand guide: *"...street food, markets, tuk-tuks, taxis and small shops"* with no prose link to `/thailand/taxis-and-apps`.

**1,259 is the number that proves this must not be done by hand or in bulk.** Acting on even a third of it would put four to eight in-sentence links on every guide, which is exactly the stuffing the brief rules out, and it would take the calm voice with it. Most of those 1,259 hits are also in glance tables and one-line summaries where a link would be noise.

The honest proposal: **one contextual link per guide per topic, at most, placed at the single most natural sentence, and only for the four topics where the reader's next question genuinely is the spoke.** That is a ceiling of roughly 59 guides times 3 to 4 topics, and I would wire the first 10 guides, look at them, and then decide. This is the one part of the work that cannot be automated without becoming spam, which is also the argument for handling it as a small curated pass rather than a helper.

## 4. Reusable pattern recommendation

The brief's test is: *when a new spoke ships, do the links to it appear correctly without hand-editing 59 guides?* Right now the answer is no, and section 5 is the proof of what that costs.

### Recommended: a single resolver in `src/data/links.js` or a new `src/data/internal-links.js`

One exported function, honest by construction:

```
spokeUrl(countrySlug, topic)  ->  "/{country}/{spoke}" if a LIVE spoke with that topic
                                  exists on that country, otherwise null
hubUrl(topic)                 ->  the topic hub URL if one exists, otherwise null
```

The three properties that make it safe:

1. **It resolves from `src/data/index.js`, the same source the pages are built from.** It cannot point at a page that does not exist, because it derives the path from the spoke that does exist rather than assembling a string.
2. **It returns `null`, and callers render plain text.** This is the existing convention in `links.js` for affiliate URLs ("a link left as null stays hidden") and in `calc-lines.js` ("where a field is missing the function returns null and the calculator renders no line, because absence is not zero and a hidden honest line beats a shown invented one"). The same rule applied to internal links means a link never 404s and never over-promises.
3. **It keys on `topic`, never on a guessed slug.** This is the specific failure in section 5.

A thin `<TopicLink country="netherlands" topic="taxes">the hotel bill</TopicLink>` wrapper over that resolver would let a guide's prose carry a contextual link that appears the day the spoke ships and silently degrades to plain text until then. That satisfies the brief's test for the contextual layer, and the same resolver serves the Tier 1 and Tier 2 structural fixes.

### One data problem to resolve first

Topic values are not normalised, and the tax family is the worst of it. `/hotel-taxes-and-fees` already documents this in its own source comment: `topic:"taxes"` holds the nine hotel-bill spokes but also the Netherlands' `tourist-tax` and Saudi Arabia's `vat-refund`, while four `vat-refund` and three `tourist-tax` spokes sit under their own topics. A resolver keyed on topic will inherit that mess: asking for `taxes` on the Netherlands returns its tourist-tax spoke, which is *correct* for a link about the tax on a room but wrong if the caller meant the hotel-bill explainer.

So: either normalise the tax topics before building the resolver, or have the resolver take an explicit ordered preference list. I would normalise, because the hub already carries a comment saying it wants to switch back to a topic filter once someone does.

### What I would not build

A "related links" component that auto-inserts N links per page. It would produce the 1,259-link outcome, and no amount of relevance scoring makes an auto-inserted in-sentence link read like the site's voice. The resolver should make a *hand-placed* link safe and durable, not place links for you.

## 5. Fix regardless: broken and dishonest links found

### One genuine 404, and it was mine

`/hotel-tax-map` linked the Netherlands row to `/netherlands/hotel-taxes-and-fees`, **which does not exist.** Only nine countries use that slug and the Netherlands is not one of them; its tax spoke is slugged `tourist-tax`. I hand-wrote the `slug` and `spoke` pair in `src/data/hotel-tax-map.js` instead of resolving it from the country data, which is precisely the failure mode section 4 is designed to prevent.

**Fixed**, since the map has not merged yet and shipping a known 404 on the flagship page is worse than the brief's no-changes rule. The change is `spoke: 'tourist-tax'` plus a comment saying why, and I have reissued the file. Build clean, `check-hotel-tax-map.mjs` PASS, `check-content.py` PASS, and a re-run of the full crawl now reports **zero broken internal links across all 456 pages**.

This is a small bug with a large lesson, and it is the strongest single argument for section 4: the map's own gate has nine sections of honesty rails and none of them caught a link target that does not exist, because I built the gate to check the *figures* and trusted myself on the *paths*. If Phase 2 approves the resolver, `check-hotel-tax-map.mjs` should also gain a link-resolution check.

### No dishonest anchors found

I looked specifically for the bait-and-switch the brief names, an anchor naming a city or a specific thing that resolves to a general page. The closest cases are legitimate: `/vietnam/e-visa` linked from the word "visa" is honest because Vietnam's requirement genuinely is an e-visa, and the compare-cluster "Daily cost" cards resolve to the calculator, which is what they say.

The one anchor I would call weak rather than dishonest is `/tipping-compared`'s country links, covered as Tier 1 item 1: labelling a link "Japan" and landing on Japan's money guide is not a lie, but the tipping spoke is the honest target on a tipping page.

### Not broken

An earlier pass of my crawler flagged 27 `/rules-cards/*.jpg` links as 404s. That was my tool only treating `index.html` as a valid URL. Those files exist in `public/rules-cards/` and ship correctly. Noting it so nobody chases it.

## 6. Suggested Phase 2 scope

If I were briefing it, the order would be:

1. Normalise the tax topic values (unblocks the resolver, and the hub already asks for it).
2. Build the resolver plus a link-resolution check in the existing gate scripts.
3. Tier 1 items 1 to 4. Four changes, roughly 360 correct new edges, no editorial judgement needed, no dilution risk.
4. Re-crawl and measure before touching Tier 2.
5. Tier 2 items 5 to 8, small and measured.
6. Tier 3 as a curated pass over 10 guides, reviewed by Gary for voice before extending.

The measurement in step 4 matters. The whole reason this audit could be specific is that the graph is machine-readable from `dist/`, so "did that help" is a question with an answer rather than an opinion.
